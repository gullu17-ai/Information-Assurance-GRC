1. Introduction
This assignment actually builds the foundation for the Information Assurance work on a Smart Sports
Stadium. The selected organization is hypothetical venue called PakArena Smart Stadium (PASS). The
connected systems are used by stadium for ticketing, crowd management, physical access, payments,
surveillance, building operations and event services. Because these connected systems are linked together.
A cyber problem can quickly become an operational or even a public-safety problem.
The aim of this report is to understand the organization before selecting detailed security controls. It
identifies who is responsible for security and operations and lists the main assets and records realistic
threats and vulnerabilities. The final part uses a simple likelihood into impact method to rate 16 major
risks. This gives the organization a clear starting point for the later work on the policies and controls,
compliance, audit, identity and access management and privacy, business continuity, incident response
and emerging technology risks.

6. Risk Assessment Method
For Assignment 1, PASS uses a simple 5 × 5 risk model. So this is keeping the scores easy to
explain and to makes it possible to compare very different risks using one method.
6.1 Risk Formula
Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact
6.2 Likelihood Scale
Score              Rating             Meaning

1                  Rare               Would only happen in unusual conditions.

2                  Unlikely           Possible, but there are few clear opportunities.

3                  Possible           Could realistically happen during normal operations.

4                  Likely             The threat has several realistic paths and is expected to
                                      occur at some point.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 8 / 13>
5                  Almost Certain     Expected frequently or already seen repeatedly in similar
                                      environments.


6.3 Impact Scale
Score       Rating              Meaning

1           Insignificant       Little disruption and no sensitive data or safety effect.

2           Minor               Small, short-lived service impact.

3           Moderate            Noticeable outage, limited data loss or moderate
                                financial/reputation effect.
4           Major               Major service disruption, significant data exposure, financial
                                loss or event impact.
5           Severe              Could affect public safety, stop major event operations, expose
                                highly sensitive data or cause serious legal/financial harm.


6.4 Risk Rating Bands
Score                Level                 Meaning

1-4                  Low                   Manage through normal controls and monitoring.

5-9                  Medium                Needs planned controls and owner review.

10-16                High                  Needs priority treatment and management attention.

17-25                Critical              Needs urgent treatment, strong oversight and clear
                                           ownership.

7.1 Overall Risk Picture
These three risks “privileged account compromise, ransomware, and a breach of the spectator
database” receives the critical rating. Risks are rated highest because of having realistic attack
paths and they can easilty affect many systems or large personal information. As smart stadium
depends on connected services thats why most of the risk are high. The Digital signage takeover
is Medium because digital signage could damage the event experience and reputation but it is
surely less likely to stop core stadium operations or to create the same level of the safety impact
as BMS or network incident by itself.

8. Likelihood and Impact Justification
R01 - Ticketing DDoS
Likelihood 4: Ticketing platform is public and it is exposed when popular event is happening and
before it, when the attackers also know that the demand is high. Impact 4: Customers can may be
unable to buy or retrieve the tickets or support lines may be overloaded and also revenue can be
lost. It is serious but fallback processes can still be used by event if properly prepared.
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 10 / 13>
R02 - Fake or Replayed Tickets
Likelihood 3: Digital tickets are common targets for sharing, copying and resale abuse. Strong
online validation can lower the chance, but weaknesses in offline or rushed event-day checks
remain realistic. Impact 4: Large-scale misuse can cause crowding, revenue loss and disputes at
gates.
R03 - POS Malware
Likelihood 3: POS systems are attractive because they handle payments, but they are usually
more restricted than normal office computers. Impact 4: A successful compromise can expose
payment data, interrupt sales and create contractual or compliance problems.
R04 - Network Segmentation Failure
Likelihood 3: A busy stadium has many device types, temporary connections and third-party
users. A configuration mistake or weak rule can happen. Impact 5: If guest or less-trusted
networks can reach operational systems, attackers could move toward critical stadium services.
R05 - CCTV Compromise
Likelihood 3: Cameras and video platforms often include many devices and remote
administration points. Impact 4: Unauthorized viewing can expose sensitive footage and reduce
security monitoring, especially during an event.
R06 - BMS Manipulation
Likelihood 3: Building systems may include legacy components and vendor remote access, but
they are not normally directly open to the public Internet. Impact 5: Bad control of ventilation,
lighting or environmental systems during a packed event can cause serious operational and safety
problems.
R07 - False IoT Sensor Data
Likelihood 3: IoT devices can have weak authentication or firmware, especially when devices
come from different vendors. Impact 4: Wrong crowd-density or facility data may lead staff to
make poor operational decisions.
R08 - Digital Signage Takeover
Likelihood 3: Signage controllers may be managed remotely and sometimes use shared accounts.
Impact 3: Offensive or false content can damage reputation and disrupt the event, but it usually
does not directly stop core safety systems.
R09 - Cloud Misconfiguration
Likelihood 3: Cloud services change frequently and human configuration mistakes are realistic.
Impact 4: Exposed storage or permissions may leak customer or business information and
interrupt digital services.
R10 - Privileged Account Compromise
<PARSED TEXT FOR PAGE: 11 / 13>
Likelihood 4: Admin accounts are directly targeted through phishing, stolen credentials and
token theft. Impact 5: One high-privilege account may give access to several systems and allow
security controls to be changed or disabled.
R11 - Ransomware
Likelihood 4: Stadium staff, contractors and vendors use many endpoints, email accounts and
remote connections, giving attackers several entry paths. Impact 5: Ransomware can affect
ticketing, office IT, event operations and recovery at the same time.
R12 - Spectator Data Breach
Likelihood 4: The database is valuable and is connected to public ticketing and mobile services.
Impact 5: A breach may expose names, contact information, ticket history and other personal
data, causing legal, financial and reputational damage.
R13 - Third-Party Compromise
Likelihood 3: The stadium relies on ticketing, payment, maintenance and technology vendors,
but their security is not fully controlled by PASS. Impact 5: A trusted vendor account or update
channel could give an attacker indirect access to important systems.
R14 - Backup Compromise
Likelihood 3: Backups are often targeted after attackers gain administrative access. Impact 5: If
backups are lost, recovery from ransomware or a major failure may take much longer and could
affect future events.
R15 - Monitoring Failure
Likelihood 3: Log gaps and misconfigured alerts are common when systems come from many
teams and vendors. Impact 4: Attacks may continue for longer without detection, increasing the
damage before response starts.
R16 - AI Crowd-Prediction Model Manipulation
Likelihood 3: The AI video analytics model is new and less studied than traditional IT systems,
but adversarial or spoofed camera input and undetected model drift are realistic given limited
monitoring of AI outputs. Impact 4: Wrong crowd-density or risk predictions could lead staff to
under-react to a genuine crowd-safety issue or over-react and disrupt an event, without directly
taking down core systems.

11. Summary
PakArena Smart Stadium is a useful Information Assurance case because it combines ordinary
business systems with technologies that directly support a crowded physical venue. The
organization depends on ticketing, digital identity, cloud services, networks, CCTV, smart entry
gates, BMS, IoT sensors and third-party providers. This means confidentiality, integrity and
availability are all important, but safety and operational continuity also have to be considered.
The report identified 16 important assets and 16 risks. The highest-rated risks are privileged
account compromise, ransomware and theft of spectator information. Other major concerns
include weak network separation, BMS manipulation, third-party access, backup compromise,
CCTV security, IoT data integrity and manipulation of the AI crowd-prediction model. The
governance model assigns responsibility across business leadership, IT, cybersecurity, facilities,
operations, physical security, compliance and audit. This provides a clear foundation for the next
assignments, where the risks can be treated with specific controls and then tested through
assurance, compliance and audit activities.
