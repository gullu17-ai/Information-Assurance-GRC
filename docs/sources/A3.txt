        1. Introduction
        In Assignment 3, the PakArena Smart Stadium (PASS) project moves from governance and
        compliance to incident response, recovery, and everyday protection. Ransomware, spectator data
        theft, BMS manipulation, insufficient network separation, confidential account breach, and AI video-
        analytics manipulation were among the sixteen major assets and sixteen threats found in the first
        deliverable. This project turns those earlier ideas into practical operational tactics using the same
        stadium.
        There are four primary components to the bundle. It starts by designing identity and access
        management so that users only have the access they require. Secondly, it categorizes stadium data
        such that private and public data are treated differently. Third, it establishes a disaster recovery plan
        and recovery goals for the services that are most important during an incident. Lastly, it describes a
        significant cyber event from planning to lessons discovered. The goal is to demonstrate how PASS
        would ensure the safe operation of an actual event even in the case of a significant security issue.

        2. Identity and Access Management (IAM)
        2.1 IAM Flow
        Passengers, long-term workers, temporary event personnel, security teams, engineers, administrators,
        and outside suppliers are just a few of the various users of PASS. They cannot be considered in the
        same way.For each account, the IAM method below delivers a straightforward path:
        User -> Identity -> Authentication -> Authorization -> Least Privilege -> Monitoring
        In reality, an individual must first have a recognized identity. After that, the system verifies that the
        individual truly possesses that identification. Access is then only allocated to the systems required for
        the work, according to the individual's position. Important activity is recorded so that anomalous
        access may be examined at a later time.

        2.3 Privileged Access
        Since Assignment 1 identified breached admin accounts as a top threat, privilege access stands among
        the most critical security vulnerability in PASS. Privilege access thus needs much higher level of
        control compared to the regular employee access:
             • Access on dual accounts: Employees use a general account that is used for communication
                  and task processing, as well as a privileged account that is used for completing high-level
                  tasks only.
             • Permissions restrictions: Access on high level is provided to those systems that an
                  administrator manages.
             • Required approval: Any changes to BMS, IAM, cloud, network configuration, or other
                  systems associated with backups can be made only using an authorized privileged account.
             • Time-restricted access: Access for vendor personnel is provided temporarily and then
                  becomes inactive automatically after completion of the task/contract.
             • Logging: Every administrative access along with important changes to any system is sent
                  automatically to SOC/SIEM for monitoring.
             • No single login: Admin accounts that serve general purposes should be phased out. “Break
                  Glass” accounts should always be restricted, and every use reported immediately.

        2.4 Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
        MFA follows a password with an additional check. Employee remote access, cloud administration,
        email, IAM administration, VPN access, and all vendor accounts should all require it. Furthermore, in
        cases where the user falls within the stadium network, the privileged accounts require the
        authentication process to be carried out. In activities that pose no threats to security, the standard login
        can be used by spectator accounts, but for more sensitive ones, an extra authentication layer is
        required.

        2.5 Access Review

        2.6 Vendor Access
        Third party organizations play a role in ticketing, payment processing, Internet access, maintenance of
        events, technology, but they cannot have unlimited access. Every vendor account needs to have a
        sponsor from within PASS who will clearly state the purpose, end date, and MFA of the account. The
        remote access is allowed through the official and designated VPN or gateway only. Also, access to
        BMS, CCTV, cloud management, or any other important system is always logged. When the process
        is done, the account is disabled.

        4.3 Recovery Order
        PASS needs to be careful during a blackout; they shouldn’t restore everything all at once. The first
        step is restoring the systems that help people get into and move around the building, identify
        themselves, communicate, and operate the facility. Once these systems are restored, the others will
        follow.
        1. Internal network, IAM and core security access.
        2. Smart turnstiles, ticket database and essential event-control services.
        3. CCTV, BMS and operational monitoring.
        4. Online ticketing, POS and other commercial services.
        5. AI analytics, public Wi-Fi and lower-priority services.

        4.4 Backup and DR Testing
        Only when PASS is able to recover from a backup is it helpful. There should be a DR test done by IT
        a minimum of twice a year and monthly sample restores performed. There also needs to be a check of
        the most recent backup status, emergency accounts, failover contacts, and recovery documentation
        availability before any major event occurs. Results are documented so that recurring issues may be
        resolved rather than being found again during an actual occurrence.

        5. Major Incident Scenario: Ransomware During a Stadium
        Event
        5.1 Scenario
        The simulated incident is based on R11 from Assignment 1, where ransomware was rated Critical. On
        the morning of a sold-out match, an event coordinator opens a convincing email attachment. The
        workstation is infected and the attacker uses stolen credentials to move into the internal network.
        Several office systems and shared files begin to encrypt. The ticketing support service becomes
        unstable, staff cannot open some operational documents, and unusual authentication activity appears
        in the SIEM. This means that the turnstiles and the BMS cannot be regarded as part of the office
        system that has been affected by the malware.
        The aim here is not only to get rid of malware. PASS should continue to ensure that fans get into the
        stadium safely, that there is no contamination of evidence and that important systems are restored.

        5.2 Incident Response Flow
              ●     Preparation
              ●     Detection
              ●     Analysis
              ●     Containment
              ●     Eradication
              ●     Recovery
              ●     Lessons Learned

        5.3 Preparation
          •       Keep an incident response contact list for SOC, IT, operations, physical security, facilities,
                  management and key vendors.
          •       Maintain tested offline/immutable backups for important servers and configurations.
          •       Send endpoint, IAM, firewall, cloud and server logs to the SIEM.
          •       Have the emergency procedures in place for validating tickets and gates in case of a failure of
                  the IT services.
          •       Train staff on phishing awareness and have MFA in place for remote and privileged access.
          •       Have an incident evidence folder and document who makes decisions.

        5.4 Detection
        The SIEM notices abnormal and failed admin logins, users notify files with unusual extensions, and
        the endpoint shows quick changes to files. All these alarms are reported to the SOC. Rather than
        dealing with each alarm separately, the monitoring staff correlate all the notifications and generate a
        single critical incident. Since the incident happened on the day of the incident, Stadium Operations
        were informed immediately.

        5.5 Analysis
        Access to OT or safety-related network, the system whose account information was accessed, the user
        account and device that first logged in, and whether there was a copying of data before encryption is
        checked for by the response team. Endpoints, IAMs, firewalls, VNPs, servers, and cloud services log
        data is gathered.

        5.6 Containment
        ● Isolate the computer terminals and affected servers from the network to ensure that the issue does
        not spread further.
        ● Disable the accounts of those who have been infected, close all their sessions, and change the
        passwords of elevated accounts that could be affected.
        ● Block all identified IP addresses, sites, and suspicious activity on your firewall, email filters, and
        antivirus software.
        ● Ensure that your physical security system and building management system are disconnected while
        carrying out the investigation.
        ● Use your contingency ticket checking system if your primary system gets affected.
        ● Take forensic images of the infected computers before cleaning and re-imaging them.

        5.7 Eradication
        IT gets rid of the malware, patches up the security loophole, and installs any necessary updates on the
        software. Instead of just removing suspicious files, the affected computers are entirely erased and
        reinstalled using clean system images. Any passwords, security tokens, and API keys used by the
        compromised accounts are changed. The security team also checks all the systems that haven’t been
        affected by any problems for the presence of threats.

        5.8 Recovery
        The restoration of systems follows a recovery priority list as provided for in the business continuity
        plan. The IT department begins by ensuring that network and identity services have been adequately
        secured before restoring the ticketing tools and the critical services using backups. Monitoring is done
        immediately after integration into the network, and full restoration can only occur if security experts
        declare the environment as being stable and malware-free.
        In case the initial restoration cannot be done within specified target timelines, operations will be
        moved to the backup disaster recovery center. Throughout the whole process, recovery teams keep
        track of total downtime and data losses.

        5.9 Lessons Learned
        Within a few days, team leads across IT, security, operations, facilities, and management meet to
        analyze the breach. They evaluate how the phishing email slipped through, how the intruder navigated
        the network, whether MFA and network isolation held up, and if backups were recovered on schedule.
        Every fix and improvement identified during the review is assigned directly to a named owner with a
        firm deadline, ensuring real accountability rather than vague suggestions.

        7. Summary
        The previous PASS risk analysis assignment has been incorporated into this current Assignment 3.
        Due to the reason that access remains open to employees, administrators, and vendors under RBAC,
        IAM strategy adopts a path Identification, Authentication, Authorization, Least Privilege, and
        Monitoring. Considering the account compromise was among the major risks in the previous PASS
        assignment, privileged access, MFA, and periodic review becomes a first priority.
        In order to ensure the equal protection for all the data with the help of PASS, a data classification
        process classifies Public, Internal, Confidential, Restricted, and Critical information. For recovery
        objectives for the services which are important in case of an incident, BC/DR framework follows the
        recovery path Primary -> Replication -> Backup -> DR site. Lastly, ransomware is an example where
        PASS's reaction against the readiness, detection, evaluation, containment, elimination, recovery and
        learning from major incidents comes into action.
